RESOLUTION NO.
A RESOLUTION WAIVING FORMAL BIDDING REQUIREMENTS AND
AUTHORIZING PAYMENT TO SKYCOP, INC., A SOLE SOURCE
VENDOR, FOR THE PURCHASE OF LICENSE PLATE READER
CAMERAS FOR THE NORTH LITTLE ROCK POLICE DEPARTMENT;
AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.
WHEREAS, Ark. Code Ann. § 14-58-303 requires City purchases exceeding the amount
of $35,000 to follow statutory procedures of local advertisement and opening of sealed bids which
may only be waived in exceptional situations where bidding is deemed not feasible or practical;
and
WHEREAS, the North Little Rock Police Department ("NLRPD") is in need of license
plate reader ("LPR") cameras, which are mounted on poles that capture the numbers and letters of
license plates on still or moving vehicles; and
WHEREAS, all LPR camera vendors require use of their specific cameras in order to be
compatible with their system; and
WHEREAS, the NLRPD uses the City of Little Rock's LPR Server, who uses SkyCop, Inc.
as their LPR camera reader; and
WHEREAS, the City Council has previously authorized the waiver of formal bidding
requirements for the purchase of LPR cameras from SkyCop, Inc.; and
WHEREAS, the NLRPD is requesting the purchase and installation of eleven (11)
additional cameras from SkyCop, Inc. at a total price of Ninety Nine Thousand Eight Hundred
Thirty Four and 62/100 Dollars ($99,834,62) including tax, in order to be compatible with the
LRPD's LPR server (see Quote from Skycop, Inc. attached hereto as Exhibit A); and
WHEREAS, the City has applied for the Bureau of Justice Assistance (BJA) Northern and
Middle States Rural Law Enforcement Assistance Program ("Program"), which will reimburse the
purchase of the cameras and associated equipment, with no matching funds required from the City
(see Program Overview and Application attached hereto as Exhibit B.)
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF
NORTH LITTLE ROCK, ARKANSAS:
SECTION 1: That formal bidding is hereby waived in connection with the purchase of
LPR cameras from SkyCop, Inc. for a total amount of Ninety Nine Thousand Eight Hundred Thirty
Four and 62/100 Dollars ($99,834.62), contingent on funding through the BJA Northern and
Middle States Rural Law Enforcement Assistance Program.

Program Overview and Application

Small, rural, and tribal law enforcement agencies in the northern and middle region of the United Sates represent a
significant portion of this nation's law enforcement. The large geographical area, coupled with the general challenges of
limited staffing, equipment, training, and technical assistance, make the need for additional resources critical for the
success of these agencies.
The Bureau of Justice Assistance (BJA) Northern and Middle States Rural Law Enforcement Assistance Program is a new
initiative designed to provide resources through subawards to small, rural, and tribal law enforcement agencies located in
the northern and middle regions of the United States. This initiative will support efforts in addressing precipitous
increases in all types of crime unique to this region, to include human trafficking, sexual assault, extorsion, gang activity,
murder, drug trafficking, and other forms of violent crime.
Program Purposes
The purposes for the subawards under this program are to:
Improve the agency's investigative, intelligence, and/or interdiction capabilities.
Enhance information sharing, including investigative and intelligence data sharing with other agencies.
Enable agencies to participate in projects within the northern and middle region of the United States.
Assist in projects/initiatives unique to an agency or its region.
Improve dedicated communications capabilities.
Provide specific technical assistance, such as hardware/software, specialized equipment, and peer assistance.
Provide support for real-time crime centers or intelligence centers that are either established or in development.
Availa' ie Funding
Funding will be made available to law enforcement agencies within the northern and middle region of the United States
through a reimbursed subaward process. Agencies may request up to $150,000 in funding for equipment and items that
support the program purposes outlined above, including, but not limited to, the items listed below:
K -9 -unit support, (e.g., K -9s, K-9 and handler training,
K-9 maintenance, and K-9 unit vehicle modifications)
Computers/laptops and peripherals (communications
cards are not covered)
In -car video camera systems
Automated license plate readers
Moving radars
3D scanner systems
Narcotic/explosive detection equipment
Videoscope inspection systems
Evidence collection/processing kits
Driver's license scanners
Records management systems
Computer -aided dispatch systems
Citation, crash, and mapping systems
Other items, as requested
Agencies should only request funding for the specific amount needed to obtain the requested item(s). Requests
cannot exceed $150,000 per agency. Agencies may submit one or more applications. However, the combined requested
amount cannot exceed the $150,000 cap. For example, a group of interdiction teams from one law enforcement agency
can only request $150,000 combined for the entire agency.

To be considered for this program, applicants must meet the following eligibility requirements:
Be a small, rural, or tribal law enforcement agency located within one of the following states: Alaska, Arkansas,
Colorado, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Louisiana, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska,
Nevada, North Dakota, Oklahoma, Oregon, South Dakota, Utah, Washington, Wisconsin, or Wyoming.
Serve a population size of 100,000 or less.
Have an application that meets one or more of the program purposes listed earlier.
Only request eligible items (items listed in the "What Is Not Covered" section should not be requested).
Have permission from the agency's governing authority to receive this award.
Be able to provide necessary training to personnel to use and implement the purchased resources, if applicable.
Be able to comply with post -award reporting requirements.
Applicants must meet the above eligibility requirements; submit a completed application; and provide timely follow-up
documentation, if requested.
As previously stated, it is imperative that the agency have the appropriate approvals from their governing body to accept
an award under this program. Also, if selected, the agency must comply with post -award reporting requirements.
Post -Award Reporting Requirements: For BJA reporting purposes, award recipients will be contacted by a
representative from the National White Collar Crime Center (NW3C) who will gather information regarding the
implementation of the award and how it benefits the agency. This communication will help to promote successful
implementations to the law enforcement field. Recipient agencies are required to submit responses to NW3C or allow a
phone interview to be performed to share details of the implementation and identify any benefit to the entity and the
administration of justice and public safety.
GZeview Process
Applications are pre-screened for eligibility and completeness. Applicants will be contacted to ensure that any missing
information is completed prior to BJA consideration. Eligible and completed applications will be forwarded to BJA for
review and approval.
As noted earlier, funding is available through a reimbursement process through subawards. Once all applications are
received and screened and the application period has been closed, BJA will undergo a competitive review process. With
law enforcement agencies from twenty-three states eligible for this program, the competitive review may take several
months. Selected subaward recipients will then be contacted for follow-up documentation.
BJA is committed to ensuring a fair and open process for granting awards. Applying does not guarantee selection. BJA
will evaluate each application to ensure that it fits within the scope of this program and is congruent with the items
covered, applicant eligibility, and the availability of funding. BJA may require additional information from the applicant to
make an award decision. Once all awards are selected, BJA will send a notification to the applicant agency of its decision
to award or not award the funding.
